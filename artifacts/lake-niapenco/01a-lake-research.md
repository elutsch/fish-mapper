## In-lake structure (by species)
Named in-lake structure is coarse: Lake Niapenco is a shallow, weedy, island-strewn reservoir with no published bathymetry or landmark-level fishing map located. Structure below is documented physical features (islands, offshoots, weedy bays, the drowned Welland River channel, the dam) tied to species by habitat.

### Largemouth Bass
- Weedy shallow bays and the many small islands and offshoots — vegetated cover across a shallow, warm reservoir is prime largemouth habitat; largemouth are ~12% of the 2017 sport-fish community and an Angler's Atlas top-caught species — https://www.biotactic.com/lake-niapenco-sport-fish-population-estimates/
- The long, narrow flooded river valley with cottage-lined offshoots — cover and structure edges throughout the impoundment — https://kayak-ontario.com/lake-niapenco-hamilton/

### Smallmouth Bass
- Harder-bottom shorelines and island edges — smallmouth are documented present (~5% of the 2017 survey; an Angler's Atlas top-caught species), but named rock structure is not publicly documented on this shallow weedy reservoir — https://www.anglersatlas.com/place/124339/lake-niapenco

### Northern Pike
- Weedy bays, offshoots, and vegetated island margins — ambush cover for pike in a shallow vegetated reservoir; present but low density in the 2017 survey — https://www.biotactic.com/lake-niapenco-sport-fish-population-estimates/

### Walleye
- Drowned Welland River channel and deeper cuts — the old river course is the most plausible walleye holding water in an otherwise shallow lake; walleye are present but low density (~0% of the 2017 sport-fish estimate) — https://www.biotactic.com/lake-niapenco-sport-fish-population-estimates/

### Black Crappie
- Brushy/weedy island edges, offshoots, and back bays — crappie relate to submerged wood and weed edges; black crappie are ~7% of the 2017 community (white crappie, out of scope, dominate at ~76%) — https://www.biotactic.com/lake-niapenco-sport-fish-population-estimates/

### Yellow Perch
- Weed edges and mid-depth flats — perch school the vegetation; both a panfish target and forage for pike/walleye — https://www.anglersatlas.com/place/124339/lake-niapenco

## Notable facts (defensible claims)
- Lake Niapenco is the largest inland lake in the Niagara Peninsula watershed, created in 1971 when the Welland River was dammed at Binbrook for summer low-flow augmentation and seasonal flood control. — https://www.openwaterpedia.com/wiki/Lake_Niapenco
- A 2017 mark-recapture study by Biotactic for the NPCA estimated the reservoir's sport-fish community at ~27,149 fish and found it dominated by white crappie (~76%), with largemouth bass (~12%), black crappie (~7%), and smallmouth bass (~5%); northern pike and walleye were a negligible share of the estimate. — https://www.biotactic.com/lake-niapenco-sport-fish-population-estimates/
- Surface area ~168 ha (~415 acres). — https://www.anglersatlas.com/place/124339/lake-niapenco
- Binbrook Conservation Area / Lake Niapenco is a well-known Hamilton-area fishing hot spot that hosts an annual spring fishing derby and an annual ice-fishing derby. — https://npca.ca/parks-recreation/conservation-areas/binbrook/ice-fishing.htm
- A fish-consumption advisory applies: the water carries PFAS, mercury, and PCBs — consult Ontario's Guide to Eating Ontario Fish. — https://npca.ca/parks-recreation/conservation-areas/binbrook/ice-fishing.htm
